The observatory features one public telescope and a narrow staircase that leads to the dome. During clear evenings, visitors sometimes outpace the telescope operator when reaching the summit via the stairs. If a new group arrives while others are still descending, those exiting the dome find limited room to move. This issue stems from both the queue’s location and its length.

To address this, for the next two open evenings, visitors will receive a numbered card placed in the downstairs hall. A volunteer will announce four numbers sequentially and hold back until the preceding group has reached the dome before proceeding to the next set. Those who fail to hear their initial announcement may approach the volunteer to join a subsequent group. The cards serve to order the queue; therefore, they will be handed out anonymously—no names or phone numbers attached.

A small monitor in the hall displays which numbers correspond to objects currently inside the dome and provides a brief description of each. Should clouds obscure an object for longer than ten minutes, the operator will suspend the calls and broadcast updates through the hall speaker. Following the second evening, volunteers will assess how long the longest-waiting line persisted compared to the recorded duration of the previous open evening, and they will record if the staircase remained unobstructed throughout.
